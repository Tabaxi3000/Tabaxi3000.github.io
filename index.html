About

I am a Stanford undergraduate in Mathematics and Computer Science drawn to the boundary between provable cryptography and deployed systems. I care about definitions, reductions, assumptions, and adversarial models, but I am equally interested in what happens when those guarantees are implemented inside real protocols, browsers, networks, and software stacks.

My technical background combines formal coursework in cryptography, algorithms, number theory, and quantum cryptography with practical adversarial experience through Stanford Applied Cybersecurity and CTFs. In capture-the-flag competitions, I focus on cryptography, reverse engineering, web exploitation, and post-quantum or lattice-flavored challenges that require both mathematical reasoning and implementation-level debugging.

As machine learning lead on a CURIS project with Prof. Chris Piech, I apply reinforcement learning— especially Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO)—to adaptive hearing test design. That work is not security research, but it has sharpened the same habits I value in cryptography: making assumptions explicit, testing model behavior under uncertainty, and asking when a formal objective survives real constraints.

Research questions

The question I keep returning to is: how can we design cryptographic systems whose mathematical guarantees survive the realities of deployment?

  • Post-quantum foundations. I am interested in lattice-based assumptions such as LWE and SIS, their role in modern post-quantum constructions, and the gap between hardness assumptions and practical migration.
  • Applied cryptography and protocol analysis. I want to understand how proofs, reductions, and adversarial models guide protocol design—and where those models become fragile under composition, implementation choices, or trust assumptions.
  • Empirical systems security. I am interested in how real systems fail: misconfiguration, parsing ambiguity, side channels, deployment pressure, and the engineering details that can invalidate otherwise careful designs.
  • Security automation with rigor. I use ML/RL and scripting as tools for modeling, testing, and automation, while keeping cryptographic reasoning and systems understanding at the center.
