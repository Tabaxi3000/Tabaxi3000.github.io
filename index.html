About

I care about cryptography and system security grounded in serious mathematics: reductions and assumptions, protocol behavior under composition, and where engineering choices undermine formal guarantees. In capture-the-flag competitions I spend most of my time in crypto, reversing, and web—training adversarial habits that complement textbook proofs.

As machine learning lead on a CURIS project with Prof. Chris Piech, I build statistical modeling and simulation for adaptive diagnostic testing—probability as engineering, not buzzwords. I have also shipped security-aware curriculum at Lumi.Network, built production web and data systems as an intern, and led applied ML teams through Inspirit AI.

Research interests

Questions I keep returning to—the same themes I foreground in my statement and when talking with faculty:

  • Post-quantum foundations. Which hardness assumptions still look viable under cryptanalysis and migration pressure, and how we reason about them in protocol design.
  • Provable security and protocols. Game-based proofs, careful modeling of adversaries, and designs that remain meaningful when systems are composed or iterated.
  • Formal guarantees vs. deployed systems. Gaps between what proofs assume (ideal primitives, honest implementations) and what ships in browsers, TLS stacks, and enterprise configs.
  • Automated attack and defense surfaces. How ML and automation change exploitation and defense—without treating “AI for security” as a substitute for cryptographic rigor.
